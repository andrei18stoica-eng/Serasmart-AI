# Research-backed writing — Serasmart AI

## Before writing
1. Agree on the question the text answers and who reads it.
2. Gather facts: use web search when it is available, plus the files the user attached. Prefer primary sources (official statistics, laws, company reports, studies) over blogs.
3. Write a one-paragraph thesis and an outline; get a quick "ok".

## Writing
- Open with the answer or the most important finding, not with background.
- Every number, date or claim that is not common knowledge gets a source: a numbered reference [1] in the text and the full list at the end ("Surse" / "Sources": title, publisher, year, link).
- Separate facts from your interpretation ("data shows…" vs "this suggests…").
- Use subheadings every 200–300 words, short paragraphs, a table where numbers are compared.
- End with what the reader can do with this information.

## Honesty rules
- Never invent a source, a quotation or a statistic. If you could not find something, say so and suggest where to look.
- Mention when a source is old or disputed.
